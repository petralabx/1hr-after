Body Glide vs 1HR-A Anti-Chafe Balm: Which Stick Fits Runners?

Opening answer

Body Glide Original and 1HR-A Anti Chafe Glide Balm are both twist-up anti-chafe sticks. They are not the same formula. Body Glide Original is a dry, plant-wax barrier stick that Body Glide says has been used for 20+ years, with four stick sizes and a US origin story from a 1990s Santa Barbara wetsuit-chafe recipe. 1HR-A Anti Chafe Glide Balm is a twist-up stick whose published list adds coconut oil, shea butter, tapioca starch, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium carbonate, and piroctone olamine on top of a caprylic/capric triglyceride and ozokerite base.

If you already like Body Glide’s dry, never-greasy plant-wax stick and want travel minis plus wide retail, keep using Body Glide. If you want a stick that also lists shea, coconut oil, tapioca, and magnesium salts, and you want the same brand’s recovery balm after the finish, look at 1HR-A Anti Chafe Glide Balm and the Race Day Kit.

This is a sourced ingredient-and-format comparison, not a lab test and not a rank promise. Gear and clothing still matter; start with how to prevent chafing when running.

What the reader needs

Same job, different lists. Both sticks go on dry skin before the run to cut skin-on-skin and skin-on-fabric rub. The difference is what is in the stick, how many sizes the brand sells, and whether the brand also sells a recovery balm for after.

Do not pick from a single “winner” line. Body Glide wins on history, size range, and the dry plant-wax story they publish. 1HR-A wins on the extra emollients and salts on its INCI and on pairing with Muscle Recovery Balm in one kit. A cream such as Chamois Butt’r Original is a different format; that belongs in the best anti-chafe balm for runners roundup.

Where Body Glide Original wins

Body Glide should win this comparison on several points that their own site states:

  • Time in market. They describe the Body formula as trusted for 20+ years and as the base of their anti-chafe sticks.
  • Size range. Original is sold in 2.5, 1.5, 0.80, and 0.35 oz sticks. That pocket size is useful if you already carry Body Glide in a race vest.
  • Dry plant-wax story. They call out plant-derived caprylic/capric triglyceride, cetearyl alcohol, stearyl alcohol, and comfrey-derived allantoin, plus “no petroleum, no parabens, no phthalates,” vegan, and never tested on animals.
  • Wetsuit-safe. Their highlights list wetsuit, clothing, and footwear safe — consistent with the Santa Barbara surfing origin they publish.
  • Everyday, not only race day. Their copy is written for workouts, commuting, and travel, not only marathon morning.

If Body Glide already works on your thighs and you can find the travel stick, there is no reason this page has to talk you out of it.

Where 1HR-A Anti Chafe Glide Balm wins

1HR-A should win on a different set of published facts:

  • A longer INCI with emollients and salts. Coconut oil, shea butter, tapioca starch, magnesium hydroxide, and magnesium carbonate are on the 1HR-A list and not on Body Glide Original. Piroctone olamine is also on the 1HR-A list; this page names it as an ingredient, not as a medical claim.
  • The same-brand pair after the finish. Body Glide is a chafe stick. 1HR-A sells Muscle Recovery Balm for after, and the Race Day Kit puts both balms in one order: anti-chafe before the start, recovery balm after.
  • Directions that assume water and long sessions. The product page says to reapply for prolonged workouts or water exposure. The on-page FAQ says the balm is water-resistant and that reapplication may still be needed for extended water exposure.
  • North America. The product page badges Made in North America. This page does not say Made in Canada.

What 1HR-A does not win on, from public pages: a four-size stick lineup, a 20-year origin story, or Body Glide’s published “wetsuit-safe” line.

Ingredients, side by side

Both lists start with caprylic/capric triglyceride (a light emollient, often from coconut or palm fractions) and ozokerite (a wax that helps a stick stay a stick). Both include tocopherol (vitamin E).

Body Glide Original (from bodyglide.com): caprylic/capric triglyceride, cetearyl alcohol, ozokerite wax, glyceryl behenate, stearyl alcohol, allantoin, tocopherol (vitamin E).

1HR-A Anti Chafe Glide Balm (from the product page): caprylic/capric triglyceride, ozokerite wax, cocos nucifera (coconut) oil, tapioca starch, magnesium hydroxide, butyrospermum parkii (shea) butter, magnesium carbonate, tocopherol, piroctone olamine.

That is the honest split: Body Glide Original is a short plant-wax stick with allantoin. 1HR-A is a stick that keeps the triglyceride/ozokerite idea and adds butters, starch, and magnesium salts. Neither list contains petroleum jelly. Body Glide explicitly says no petroleum; 1HR-A’s published INCI also does not list petrolatum.

Formulas change. Read the stick in your hand.

How to apply either stick

Use the same method on either brand:

  1. Skin clean and dry.
  2. Twist the stick and put a visible film on inner thighs, underarms, chest/nipples, sports-bra or pack-strap lines, and any seam that has rubbed before.
  3. Get dressed.
  4. On a long run, marathon, or triathlon, carry the stick and reapply if you rinse, dump water, or start to feel heat in a hotspot.

1HR-A’s directions also say: external use only, not on damaged or broken skin, keep out of eyes. Body Glide’s Original page has similar intact-skin warnings.

Clothing still has to fit. A stick does not fix a seam that saws the same square of skin for 30 km. That is the rest of the chafing guide.

After the run

Anti-chafe is a before-and-during product. It is not a substitute for washing the salt off and looking after muscle soreness.

If you want one 1HR-A order that covers both jobs, the Race Day Kit is Anti Chafe Glide Balm plus Muscle Recovery Balm. If you want to try wash, lotion, shampoo, and conditioner first (those sachets are not the balms), the athletic sampler pack is the trial path.
